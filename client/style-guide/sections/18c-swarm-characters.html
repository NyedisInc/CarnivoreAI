fifty eye expressions

Every 120 to 180 seconds the shared clock picks ONE visible character that is at rest (idle or finished_unread, settled for 3 s) and plays one random face on it for 1.2 to 2.5 s: only the two eyes change (shape, size, tilt, offset, one quick motion), then they return to normal. Never twice in a row, never on dead, asleep, working, a question, the startup gate or an alert, never in reduced motion, and a hidden tab pauses it. Pick any name to play it now; "cycle all" runs the fifty at 1.5 s each; the sheet below holds every face still at 28 and 64 px.

the alert ladder is a body language

The spikes are one static path per rung, drawn behind the body in the body's own color and standing on its outline, so the glyph itself looks spiky. A PRIORITY badge, when there is one, lives on the Swarm drawer tab and never on a node.

ribbons and glyphs
A working square's ribbon turns in 0.6 of the mockup's time, is wider, and wears a bright series color that is never the body's (contrastIndex). The dead gravestone is grey (--oc-text-dim) with an engraved cross and RIP (.pk-rip) on a ground line (.pk-gl).
one attribute
One data-state per square picks every pose, keyframe and shape in web/src/cplugs/huddle/swarm/characters/characters.css, the status-led.css precedent. Exactly one animation per state, held by animation-map.test.ts.
motion
Only transform and opacity animate (plus the individual translate, scale and rotate properties), and the body shape morphs ONCE per state change through SMIL, begun one frame after the svg is inserted (a morph begun in the insertion frame never plays in Chromium). One shared clock (clock.ts) writes the gaze, the blinks and the rare idle quirk; a character at rest is still. A hidden tab pauses with animation-play-state (never animation: none), stops the clock and pauses SMIL.

state
One state per square, first that applies: an open alert's rung, a fresh activity (8 seconds), question_waiting, asleep, the startup gate, the listing's activity status, else unknown. The rules are pure, in swarm/panel-state.ts.
floor
drag within a tier
A node drags to a new place inside its own tier (COMPANIES, PROJECTS, AGENTS; tier-drag.ts, #1453): the mouse lifts it after a 5 px move, a finger after a 500 ms hold. The tier grid takes .pkp-drag-on (no text selection, the neighbours slide to their new slots by transform); the node takes .pkp-drag-lift, the Street View pegman: it rises over --oc-shadow-lift, grows a little and swings from the grab point. A release inside the tier glides it into the gap (.pkp-drag-home) and it settles with a small bounce (.pkp-drag-plop); Esc or a release outside the tier slides it home. Reduced motion: no swing, no slide, no bounce. The order is one server value for every device (GET / PUT /swarm/node-order, pushed as swarm_node_order), keyed on the node, never a tmux name. Shift+click stays the lineage filter and never lifts.
never
No count, dot or ring on a node; the corner tell is the only badge. The PRIORITY count (agents whose alert is priority or higher) is the drawer tab's badge. A density change never remounts a node, since a remount replays its entry.

a node's own look: the node menu

where
The drawer's Swarm grid, the Swarm page and the org chart open it; an ordinary session's stand-in and a vacant seat have none.
reads
Every surface draws a node's shape and tile through the one resolver, shapeFor and backgroundFor in node-look.ts, passed to Character as shape and background: the drawer square, the Swarm page, the org chart, the ledger, the board faces and the chat avatar all agree. A node's own shape beats its tier's; its tile is a series color (the slot 1 to 8) mixed toward transparent drawn behind the body (data-tile, --tile).
never
Never pass a shape from the rank alone (rank= without shape={shapeFor(it)}): the node's own pick would show on one surface and not another.

common mistakes

  • A round glow behind a node. A circle is thick at the sides and thin at the corners. The halo is a rounded rectangle with the body's corner radius and equal spread.
  • A ribbon in the body's own color. Use contrastIndex, never the body's series.
  • A timer per square. The one shared clock writes the gaze and blinks; keyframes start because data-state changed.
  • Starting the morph in the insertion frame. Begin it in a following animation frame.
  • Picking a square's color anywhere but the color book. A view that calls seriesVar or a random slot breaks the company grouping; pass color={colors.of(agent)}.
  • A literal color. Body, eyes and ribbons are tokens, so every theme works.
